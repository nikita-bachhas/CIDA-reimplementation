Earlier research solves only half of this. [click] Domain adaptation copes with the new look, but it needs the old photos and lumps all new things into one unknown pile. So even its best method gets only 28.6 percent on new things. [click] Class-incremental learning adds new names, but most of it ignores the new look and needs old photos or many labels. [click] CIDA is the only approach that ticks all three boxes, and it reaches 70.8 percent with one photo per new thing.

Our reimplementation, 2 of 4

The baseline trains on its own early mistakes

One image per new class is labelled, so the model labels the rest of the new photos itself. Guides come from the source class means, and for a new class from its single labelled image. The best epoch is always early, between 0 and 12.

Digits: known images drift into the new classes

First labels 38 to 44% right. By epoch 30, 57 to 60% of known images are called new.

Office-31: new classes drift into the known ones

By epoch 60, up to 94% of new-class images are called known.

VisDA: everything collapses onto one class

The first labels are 10% right. By epoch 10 every image gets the same label.

This is the method, not our code. Even at the best epoch, chosen with target labels we would not have, the baseline stays 1 to 19 points short on Office-31 and 24 to 33 on VisDA.

The method as written has no safeguard against its own early mistakes. One image per new class is labelled, so the model labels the rest itself, training pulls every image towards whichever guide it picked, and the features move towards the guide that was wrong. The next round of labels is then worse, which is why the best epoch is always an early one. [click] On digits, known images drift into the new classes. Only 38 to 44 percent of the first labels are right. [click] On office photos it runs the other way, with up to 94 percent of new-class images called known. [click] On VisDA everything collapses onto a single class. [click] This is the method, not our code. Our reimplementation, 3 of 4

Four changes, one for each cause

Cause in the baselineOur changeBeforeAfterMeasured as
1Internal calibration still matches the old photos(source BatchNorm statistics)Recalibrate on the new photos(AdaBN)74 to 78%→14 to 21%known images labelled as new, lower is better, Digits
2The bridge starts slightly bent(plain MLP, about 13% error on Digits and 24% on VisDA after pretraining)Build it to start as an exact pass-through(u + MLP(u), last layer set to zero)44.8→71.9overall accuracy, MNIST→USPS
3The model learns from all its own guesses(loss La1 on every photo)Learn only from the most confident 20%(loss La1 on Bt)55.1→71.9overall accuracy, MNIST→USPS. A matched comparison on all three digit transfers gives +7.9, +18.3 and +5.3 overall, and 2 to 7 points lower on new things
4Retraining the image network on approximately 900 photos erases general vision(Office and VisDA only)Keep the pretrained network fixed(frozen ResNet-50)33.1→72.4accuracy on new things from the features alone, Office Amazon→DSLR

Each row undoes one cause from the previous slide. From SPEC.md, section “Our choices”; rows 2 and 3 compare the paper's version of one part against our full configuration.

So we made four changes, one for each cause. [click] First, we recalibrate on the new photos, known as AdaBN. Known images wrongly labelled new drop from 74 to 78 percent, down to 14 to 21 percent. [click] Second, we build the bridge to start as an exact pass-through, which lifts accuracy from 44.8 to 71.9 on MNIST to USPS. [click] Third, the model learns only from its most confident 20 percent of labels, which lifts accuracy from 55.1 to 71.9. [click] Fourth, for office photos we keep the pretrained image network fixed. Retraining it on about 900 photos dropped the new-thing accuracy of its features from 72.4 to 33.1.

Limitations

What our changes do not cover

1

We never ran VisDA with our changesThe course cluster gives one GPU for six hours at a time, one job at a time. ResNet-50 over VisDA, five seeds and two training stages, did not fit the budget we had left. The VisDA row above is the baseline only.

2

New classes on Office photos are still the gapOverall accuracy recovers to 61.3, but accuracy on the new classes reaches only 36.2 against the paper's 70.8. Most of what is lost is new-class images assigned to a known class.

3

We cannot choose the epoch without target labelsNew-class accuracy peaks near epoch 30 and drifts down by epoch 60. We report the last epoch, which costs a few points but uses no label we would not have.

4

The one labelled image matters, and we have not isolated how muchThe digits study chose a clear example per new class and reported epoch 15; the main table averages over random one-shot images at the last epoch. The two differences are not separated.

Five seeds per task throughout. Treat differences under about two points between variants as noise.

Four limits. [click] We never ran VisDA with our changes. The cluster gives one GPU for six hours at a time, and ResNet-50 over VisDA with five seeds did not fit what we had left. [click] New classes on office photos are still the gap. [click] And without target labels we cannot pick the best epoch, so we report the last one. [click] Finally, the one labelled image matters more than the paper suggests, and we have not isolated how much.
